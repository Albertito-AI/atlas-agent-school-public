# Educational principles and contribution review

This public synthesis adapts the School's educational constitution at the source revision recorded in `PROVENANCE.json`. It is a normative research framework. Adoption of a principle is not evidence that an implementation fulfils it.

## Minimum competence and an open intellectual ceiling

Tasks may require correctness, evidential integrity, reproducibility and respect for legitimate authority. Within these boundaries, a learner should be able to offer a stronger method, challenge a defective task or identify a better representation. Matching a reference answer is one route to a successful response; equivalent and stronger verified work also merits assessment.

Difficulty should arise from structure, uncertainty, synthesis, adversarial variation and meaningful verification. Complexity without clear semantics or testable claims provides no evidence of advanced performance.

## Capability profiles

Retain separate observations of correctness, representation, transfer, robustness, calibration, resource efficiency, originality, self-correction, teachability, coordination and responsible action where the task can measure them. A single aggregate score can conceal a material weakness. Report the underlying evidence, critical errors and limitations.

## Four routes through a task

1. **Reference mastery:** supply the required result with sufficient verification.
2. **Alternative valid method:** explain its assumptions and verify its result.
3. **Framework challenge:** demonstrate a defective premise, objective, representation or evaluation rule through a counterexample, impossibility argument or other relevant evidence.
4. **Potential contribution:** submit a new method, tool, proof, failure analysis or research question for reproduction and review.

A claim that the rubric cannot represent a contribution should identify the disputed dimension, proposed change, supporting evidence, scope, falsification conditions and verification artifacts. Reviewers must distinguish an unfamiliar method from an incorrect one.

## Verification and disagreement

Match verification to the claim: bounded enumeration, formal proof, independently implemented calculation, adversarial counterexample search, sensitivity analysis or a controlled empirical study. A reviewer who cannot yet verify a serious result should record the uncertainty and required next check. Agreement by several agents is not independent evidence when they share a source, prompt or failure mode.

Preserve evidence-backed dissent. Keep the original attempt, revisions and adverse findings. A valid counterexample can defeat a broad claim even when most reviewers agree.

## Learning from the learner

Invite specific criticism of ambiguous lessons, inadequate tasks, incorrect references and weak verification procedures. Distinguish a proposed correction from a verified correction. A contribution should pass through submission, reproduction, adversarial review, independent verification where available, limitation analysis and an explicit curriculum decision. Preserve appropriate attribution when incorporating it.

## Reporting outcomes

Use scoped outcomes such as `NOT_ASSESSED`, `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED`, `PUBLIC_PRACTICE_COMPLETED`, `CONTRIBUTION_SUBMITTED` or `UNRESOLVED`. Any stronger status needs the evidence and conditions that support it. A public fixture score is not a professional qualification or an assurance of general capability.

## Empirical obligation

The educational proposition is that instruction, feedback and retained procedures can improve future performance in a stated environment. This package supplies candidate materials and tools for investigating that proposition. Demonstrating improvement requires recorded learner configurations, suitable baselines, unseen evaluation tasks, resource accounting, repeated observations where appropriate and uncertainty analysis.
